# Board games cheat sheet

## Mechanics glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Worker placement | You assign limited pawns to action spaces; once a space is taken, others can't use it |
| Deck building | You start with a small deck and add stronger cards as you play |
| Engine building | You build combos that give increasing returns each round |
| Drafting | Players pick a card from a hand, then pass the rest along |
| Area control | You compete for majority or influence in regions of the board |
| Tile placement | You place tiles to build a shared map or personal board |
| Hidden roles | Some players secretly belong to a team (social deduction) |
| Cooperative | Players win or lose together against the game |
| Euro-style | Resource management and points; little direct conflict or luck |
| Roll-and-write | Roll dice, then record the results on a sheet |

## Weight (complexity) rough guide

BoardGameGeek ratings are community-voted on a scale of 1–5:

- **Around 1–2**: light; learnable in minutes (family and party games).
- **Around 2–3**: medium; a rules explanation of 10–20 minutes.
- **Around 3.5 and up**: heavy; expect a long teach and long sessions.

Check a game's page for its weight rating, player-count recommendations and playing time.

## Teaching a game

1. State the goal and how the game ends first.
2. Walk through a turn, in order.
3. Cover the exceptions and edge cases only once the basic turn is clear.
4. Play the first round open, with hints; the first game is a learning game.
5. Have a rules summary on hand, and look up disputes in the rulebook instead of arguing them.

## Choosing what to play

- Match the **player count**: many games are best at some numbers and weak at others.
- Match the **time** you have; plan for the first game to take longer than the box says.
- Match the **group**: competitive, cooperative, or mostly social.
- For new players, start with something light and build up.

## Care and storage

- Store boxes flat so the bottoms don't warp; avoid stacking heavy games on thin boxes.
- Use small zip bags for components and card sleeves for heavily shuffled decks.
- Keep games out of direct sun and damp.
